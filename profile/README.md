## Philosophy

We believe reading is a ritual.

Most manga apps treat content as data to be displayed.  
We treat it as an experience to be felt.

<br />

> **Interfaces should disappear.**  
> The reader enters the story — not the app.

<br />

- Immersion is not a feature. It is the foundation.
- Motion communicates. Stillness speaks. Both are designed.
- Speed is a form of respect.
- Atmosphere is as important as content.
- Modern Android can be extraordinary. We intend to prove it.
- Design should amplify emotion — never compete with it.

## Design Language

Sarimo's visual identity is built on a single idea: **depth through restraint.**

<br />

We draw from the aesthetics of liquid glass — surfaces that are present, but transparent. Layers that suggest space without demanding attention. Shadow as atmosphere. Light as texture.

<br />

**What you'll find in Sarimo UI:**

<br />

```
Cinematic transitions         — pages turn, scenes breathe
Layered surface depth         — glass, blur, and ambient shadow
Floating navigation           — controls that surface and recede
Distraction-free reading      — a canvas, not a dashboard
Typography-first layouts      — type as the primary design element
Smooth motion systems         — nothing snaps; everything flows
Ambient backgrounds           — color derived from content
Minimal controls              — only what is needed
```

<br />

Every element earns its place.  
What remains is intention.

Sarimo is built native — because native is where craft lives on Android.

- **Kotlin** — idiomatic, expressive, modern
- **Jetpack Compose** — declarative UI that moves with us
- **Modern Android Architecture** — clean, testable, observable
- Open-source foundations, chosen with care

We don't add dependencies. We evaluate them.

## Open Source

Sarimo is being built in the open — not as a marketing decision, but as a design one.

<br />

We believe that transparency produces better software.  
That community surfaces what solo vision misses.  
That indie development thrives when it shares.

<br />

This is not a corporate open-source initiative.  
It is a small team building something they care deeply about,  
inviting others who care too.

<br />

If you believe Android deserves better experiences —

you belong here.
